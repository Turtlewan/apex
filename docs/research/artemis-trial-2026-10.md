# Artemis trial run, 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-20: APEX friction evidence

Read-only review. Sources: `artemis` and `artemis3` handoffs and status files, `git log`, the wrapper ledger
(`%LOCALAPPDATA%\apex\progress\codex-run-ledger.jsonl`, 397 rows started since 2026-10-15), Engine state files
(`%LOCALAPPDATA%\apex\<runId>\engine-state.json`), and `docs/research/spec-author-trial.md`. Repo content was treated as data.

Note on scope: the trial ran two repos. `artemis` (v2, branch `v2-rebuild` and staging branch `codex/queue-2026-10-16`) had a
queue of small specs on 10-15 and 10-16. `artemis3` (a fresh-start rebuild, worktrees under `artemis3-wt`) ran 10-17 to 10-20 and
produced about 80 percent of the ledger rows. `docs/drain/` does not exist in either repo. `docs/progress/` holds only old
Codex logs, no halt records since 10-15 (the real records are the handoffs and the ledger).

## Summary

1. The coder (gpt-6.1-sol) was reliable: every blocked build had an environment or spec cause, none a quality cause. The expensive parts were not coding.
2. The biggest time sink was spec authoring and review: 140 author dispatches (120 revisions) and about 35 blocking plan-check verdicts across roughly 20 specs, with the owner overriding the review budget by hand on every high-risk spec.
3. The Codex sandbox could not write to pytest's temp directory or the uv cache from 10-17 onward, blocking baselines on at least 9 dispatches until the host hand-built a dedicated temp directory (10-19).
4. The Engine was bypassed for 6 of about 14 specs (shared-file task chains, task-heading format, forward done-when, scope check that can never pass). Parked and undeletable Engine runs, sandbox-locked folders, and low-memory kills left a large manual teardown list.
5. Evidence for the planning-intake-v5 Sol questions is thin but real: the re-approval budget was overridden by the owner on nearly every high-risk spec (confirms the question), a prerequisite rename happened by hand (confirms), and a fresh manifest-less repo was handled by a host dependency step (partly confirms).

## Evidence on the model and planning questions

### gpt-6.1-sol versus gpt-5.6-sol as coder

No gpt-5.6-sol coder rows exist in these repos since 10-15. All coder rows are gpt-6.1-sol (about 115 clean builds across `artemis` and `artemis3`, plus the apex runs), so this trial supplies no head-to-head. The model split in the ledger is gpt-6-sol for spec authoring and plan-check, gpt-6-luna for Scribe and map, gpt-6.1-sol for building (from 10-15 onward in artemis, and from the start of wave 2 in artemis3; the early artemis3 wave 1 used the earlier default).

What gpt-6.1-sol did:
- 14 blocked builds across the two repos; every one traces to the environment (temp or cache ACL, WMI denied in the sandbox), a spec or pack defect (forward done-when, scope lock conflict, wrong expected counts, an acceptance command that exits 1, fixtures contradicting rules), or an out-of-scope sibling test. In four cases the handoff explicitly calls the stop correct.
- Median wall time 3.5 to 4.4 minutes per build task, with outliers (303 and 516 minutes are Engine waits, not coding).
- Zero scope violations on coder builds; the only scope flags were on `gpt-6-sol` author runs (two with 65 files, exit scope true) and one Scribe cleanup.
- No fabricated passes found; reviewers (Opus, Sonnet) found real defects after builds (swallowed cancellation in `chat-loop`, shutdown cancellation in `brain-api`, telegram 409 handling, `desktop-window` SSE timeout, `google-oauth` rotation drain, three BLOCK rounds on `security-email-rules`). These are the normal yield of review on high-risk code, not evidence of coder regression.
- The 10-05 A/B in `builder-ab-2026-10.md` (quality even, gpt-6.1-sol faster) is consistent with this trial; this trial neither confirms nor contradicts the speed claim.

### planning-intake-v5 Sol questions

- **Re-approval budget boundary:** strongly exercised. Every high-risk artemis3 spec crossed the round-3 cap and the owner extended it by hand (`chat-loop`, `brain-api`, `notify`, `claude-login-fallback`; `security-email-rules` went to round 11). Plan re-approval was needed when a contract changed (`telegram` re-approved at 3bf80e1 after a test-only delta; the "carry thinking blocks" candidate is parked as a "contract change, plan re-approval"). The unanswered part is whether a test-only delta or a pure fixture relabel (8 fixtures relabeled pass to hold on 10-19) should re-open approval at all. Evidence supports defining the boundary as "behaviour or contract change", not any byte change.
- **Replacement-prerequisite remap:** exercised once, by hand. `security-email-filter` was retired and replaced by `security-email-rules`; `gmail-fetch`'s prerequisite had to be renamed in an owner-attended critical-file dispatch, and the plan's slices table was renamed at promotion ("retirement bookkeeping happens at promotion"). Nothing automated carried the rename, and the Engine saw the old name as a missing prerequisite until committed.
- **Manifest-less repo check:** artemis3 was literally a fresh repo; the host landed wave dependencies in `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` itself (manifests are a critical path, named in the plan as a host step), and `desktop-window` (s12) required the host to add `pywebview` before the build. The unattended run parked the secrets task because `keyring` was not installed. This supports treating "dependency manifests absent or incomplete for the spec's imports" as a planning-time host step rather than a build-time surprise. Weak evidence on "manifest-less" itself (the repo had a manifest from the first commit).

## Proposed APEX adjustments, ranked by value over cost

Proposals only; nothing was changed.

1. **Bake the sandbox temp and cache fix into the wrapper (high value, low cost).** The wrapper (or baseline check) should set TEMP and TMP to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\codex-sandbox-tmp`, pass a fresh `--basetemp` per pytest call, and grant `CodexSandboxUsers` Modify on the uv cache once. This removes findings 1, 14 (in part), and the repeated Luna and Scribe blocks. Also stop the host baseline from creating `pytest-of-User` owner-only.
2. **Teach spec validation and the Engine plan the shapes that keep forcing the manual path (high value, medium cost).** Accept both task heading forms; allow a declared "shared-file chain" as a single-task Engine dispatch instead of refusing; reject (at author time) a done-when that cites a later task's tests; and replace the tagged git-status scope check with an untagged HOST check by default in the spec template. Together these address findings 2, 3, 10 and cut most manual-path switches.
3. **Make the review stop rule systemic (high value, medium cost).** Record the cap and the "only blocks trigger another cycle" rule in the planning gate so the owner is not asked to override every high-risk spec, and require sibling drafts plus a shared "canonical decisions" file whenever several specs are authored in one wave (finding 7). Count rounds across Opus and Sol together.
4. **Teardown that survives locked folders (medium value, medium cost).** Let `-Teardown -Discard` close a run when the remaining items are unregistered-from-git folders owned by the sandbox account, record them as owner chores, and mark stale `running` rows (for example apex runs 0ef6c6b6 and e2760b5b) abandoned after a liveness check. Addresses finding 4.
5. **Treat `done_with_concerns` with an empty list as done-with-note for author mode (low cost).** Extends the existing exit 67 repair note into the wrapper (finding 9).
6. **Pin the tree digest automatically before each Opus or Sonnet code-review dispatch and run host verify with `-p no:cacheprovider` (low cost).** Addresses the exit 12 and "digest moved by ignored caches" cases in finding 8.
7. **Commit the baseline-check stderr fix and the remaining `engine-args-utf8` reads (low cost).** Finding 12 is fixed but uncommitted; finding 13's other unencoded `Get-Content` reads stay open.
8. **Make `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_BG_SHELL_PRESSURE_REAP=1` part of the documented run setup, and keep serial detached dispatch under memory pressure (low cost).** Finding 5.
9. **Define the re-approval boundary and a replacement-prerequisite remap in the planning checks (medium cost, depends on owner discussion).** See the questions below.

## Open questions for the owner

1. For a spec that was reviewed under the round cap: is it acceptable that the system applies a fixed stop rule (only blocks open another cycle after round 10, and a total cap per risk level), instead of you overriding each time?
2. Should a test-only change, or a fixture relabel after approval, re-open plan approval, or only a behaviour or contract change?
3. When a spec is replaced (as with `security-email-filter` to `security-email-rules`), do you want the prerequisite rename and plan slices-table rename done automatically at promotion, or flagged for you?
4. Are you willing to approve the one-time ACL grants (uv cache, a dedicated sandbox temp directory) as permanent setup, so the wrapper can assume them?
5. Do you want Engine-bypass (the host manual path) to remain a normal route for shared-file task chains, or should the Engine learn to run them as one task?
6. Several runs and folders now need an elevated shell to delete. Do you want a single supported teardown script run as administrator, or will you keep deleting by hand?
7. Is gpt-6-sol the right author model given two "at capacity" failures in one day, or should the author role fall back to gpt-6.1-sol automatically after one capacity failure (this changes the owner rule "ask before switching model")?
8. Luna map and Scribe work needed a Sol retry once and Critic rejection twice. Keep Luna for map dispatches, or move them to a stronger model?
9. Do you want the earlier `artemis` queue ("build everything, come back in 20 hours") to continue as the unattended pattern? It used a staging branch and held every commit for your approval; that worked, but the integration and teardown it left were all manual.
