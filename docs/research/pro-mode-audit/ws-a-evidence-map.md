## The honesty problem stated up front

We are inferring Pro's behaviour on *failure modes* from benchmarks that mostly measure something else, against *the wrong baseline*:

1. **Wrong comparand.** Almost every benchmark number we have is Pro-vs-Opus. The decision is **Flash → Pro**. Pro being 8 pp behind Opus on SWE-bench tells us little about how far Pro is *ahead of Flash* on constraint-stacking resilience.
2. **No DeepSeek-specific conflict study.** WS-3 §3 is explicit: no controlled study tested any DeepSeek model under conflicting-directive conditions. The "weak model decays exponentially" finding is inferred from Qwen-7B / Llama-Scout / Mistral, then mapped onto Flash. Mapping it *off* Flash onto Pro is a second inference layer.
3. **Benchmark ≠ failure-mode proxy.** SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench measure end-to-end task success on *clean* prompts. They are weak proxies for "does compliance survive 30 stacked omission constraints over a 25-turn session." WS-3's own evidence shows IFEval-style clean benchmarks overstate real agentic instruction-following by ~60 pp (AgentIF). So a high SWE-bench score does **not** license relaxing a constraint-stacking compensation.
4. **The thinking-mode confound.** Pro's strong numbers are `think-max`. WS-3 §1c/§3a found that *thinking* capacity is what buys conflict-resolution robustness — but `model-comparison/README.md` warns think-max inflates cost above the 3.1× sticker. So the capability that would most plausibly close these gaps is exactly the one that erodes Pro's cost advantage. Relaxing a compensation "because Pro is smart" silently assumes think-max is on.

Where a row's proxy is weak or absent, it is rated **Unknown / Low**. We do not manufacture confidence.

## Failure modes where Pro is NOT meaningfully better (must be KEPT regardless of tier)

These rows are flagged because the evidence says capability does **not** close the gap — relaxing them under Pro is unsupported or contradicted:

- **#2 Prohibition/omission-rule decay** — architectural + scale-crossing (arXiv:2604.20911); bigger model = same architecture.
- **#6 Joint-compliance collapse** — capability raises the floor but ceiling is ~36% even for good models; remove the cause.
- **#7 Priority/hierarchy enforcement** — *explicitly* non-monotonic in capability (GPT-4o worse than 4o-mini); root cause architectural (Control Illusion). Highest-conviction keep.
- **#8 Long-horizon erosion** — quality prompting doesn't change the decay slope for *any* tested agent; Pro's 384K output may worsen it.
- **#9 Redundant-context penalty** — model-agnostic context-hygiene issue; cost penalty *grows* 3.1× on Pro.

## Bottom line

The compensations split cleanly into two classes by **why** they exist.

**Relax-safe-ish (capability-bounded failures — Pro genuinely lifts these):** the compensations that exist because Flash is a *weak end-to-end coder* — poor gap-filling under thin specs (#3), multi-file reasoning fragility (#5), and the catastrophic-floor tail of constraint stacking (#1). For these, the benchmark axis roughly matches the failure mode (Terminal-Bench/agentic for exploration, SWE-bench for multi-file), Pro's absolute scores are high, and the one near-direct Flash↔Pro signal we have (ADR-016 ~11 pp agentic gap) points the right way. Relax confidence is **Med, not High** — because the comparand is Pro-vs-Opus not Flash-vs-Pro, and the relax should be on the *defensive margin* (over-enumeration, over-fragmentation, anti-floor verbosity) rather than the lean discipline itself, which also pays for cost.

**Keep-regardless (architectural or context-hygiene failures — Pro does NOT fix these):** the compensations that exist because of how *transformer instruction-following itself* fails — prohibition-rule decay (#2), joint-compliance collapse (#6), priority-hierarchy enforcement (#7), long-horizon erosion (#8), and redundant-context penalty (#9). WS-3's strongest sources (Control Illusion, the omission-decay study) say these are architectural and either scale-crossing or *non-monotonic in capability*. A bigger DeepSeek is the same architecture; #7 even shows capability hurting. Two of these (#9 cost, #8 verbosity) get *worse* under Pro's 3.1× token price and 384K output. These stay at every tier — and the cheapest of them (removal/de-conflict for #2/#6/#7) should stay because they cost almost nothing and Pro's headroom is better spent elsewhere.

**Single most important caveat for the audit:** every "Yes" in this map quietly assumes Pro runs in a **thinking mode** — WS-3 §1c/§3a found reasoning capacity is what buys conflict resolution, and Pro's headline numbers are `think-max`. But think-max inflates cost above the 3.1× sticker (`model-comparison/README.md`). So the relax case and the cost case are in tension: the configuration that most justifies relaxing compensations is the one that most erodes Pro's reason-to-exist. Any audit row that relaxes a compensation "because Pro is capable" must state which thinking tier that assumes.
