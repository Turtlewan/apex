## Framing: what the benchmarks actually tell us

The head-to-head doc compares **V4 Pro vs Opus 4.8**, not V4 Pro vs V4 Flash. So it tells us Pro is a *near-frontier* coder (coding composite 75.9 vs Opus 76.4 — effectively tied; SWE-bench 80.6% Max vs 88.6%; agentic composite 74.0 vs 80.1 — Opus still leads agentic by ~6pp). The Flash→Pro delta is therefore inferred as **large on raw coding, smaller on long-horizon agentic orchestration**.

The WS-3 evidence is the load-bearing constraint: weak/small models show **exponential** instruction-decay even at low rule counts; frontier/stronger models show **threshold** decay (safe to ~150 rules, then collapse). The whole question is whether Pro has crossed from the exponential regime into the threshold regime. Benchmarks say Pro is near-frontier on *single-shot coding* but only mid-pack on *agentic instruction stacks* (AgentIF-style). **That asymmetry is the spine of every tag below:** raw-coding-quality compensations relax more readily than orchestration/instruction-discipline compensations.

A second hard constraint: **most of these compensations are KEEP regardless of model.** Karpathy pre-flight, surgical scope, one-hypothesis recovery, hard blocks, capture-at-halt — these are universal senior-engineer discipline. A stronger model executes them *better*; it does not stop needing them. Over-relaxing here trades a ~3.1× cost saving against shipped-defect risk on auth/data/security paths, which is a bad trade.

## Cross-cutting notes for the Pro-mode design

1. **The KEEP majority is the headline finding, not a gap.** ~80% of apex-code's compensations are universal senior-engineer discipline (verify-before-done, surgical scope, one-hypothesis recovery, conflict-surfacing, hard blocks). A stronger coder executes them better and cheaper — it does not outgrow them. Pro mode should be a *light* overlay, not a rewrite.

2. **Relaxations cluster on raw-coding-quality compensations; orchestration/instruction-discipline compensations stay.** This mirrors the benchmark asymmetry: Pro ≈ Opus on single-shot coding but lags ~6pp on agentic instruction stacks. So we loosen things that depend on *code-writing accuracy* (simplicity edits, single-reviewer-per-pass) and keep things that depend on *holding a multi-rule contract under load* (sole-workflow lock, fork routing, conflict surfacing).

3. **Every relaxation is gated, not global.** The two strongest relaxations (single-pass review, larger worker tasks) are gated on `risk` level / task independence — they ride existing escape hatches (the `risk: low` override, the independence test) rather than inventing new autonomy. This keeps the blast radius small if the Pro inference is wrong.

4. **Autonomy level is the cleaner lever than skill-text relaxation.** Much of what one might want from "Pro mode" (more multi-file autonomy, commits) is already expressible by raising L under governance. Prefer nudging the default L for Pro builds over rewriting apex-code's safety rules.
